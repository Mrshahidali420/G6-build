## The short answer

**Rockstar has not announced GTA 6's resolution, frame rate, or HDR support,
so no TV can be called "the GTA 6 TV."** What follows is general advice for
picking a good TV for any big 2026 console game on PS5 or Xbox Series X|S.
The features below (HDMI 2.1, VRR, 120Hz, game mode) help with modern console
gaming in general. Treat every specific GTA 6 performance claim you see
elsewhere with real caution.

## Why nobody can say what TV GTA 6 "needs"

GTA 6 has not been released. Nobody outside Rockstar has played it, and
Rockstar has not published technical details about how the game runs on PS5
or Xbox Series X|S. There is no confirmed frame rate, no confirmed resolution
mode, and no confirmed HDR format.

That means any article claiming GTA 6 "runs at 60fps in 4K" or "supports
Dolby Vision" is guessing, not reporting a fact. This page will not do that.
Instead, it explains the TV features that matter for console gaming in
general, in plain terms, so you can make a sensible choice regardless of what
GTA 6 turns out to need.

## The confusing terms, explained simply

These terms get thrown around a lot in TV marketing. Here is what each one
actually means, without the jargon.

**HDMI 2.1**
This is a type of HDMI cable and port that can carry more data than older
HDMI versions. It is what allows features like 4K at high frame rates and
VRR to work between a console and a TV. Both PS5 and Xbox Series X|S support
HDMI 2.1. If a TV only has HDMI 2.0 ports, you lose access to these features
even if the TV is otherwise good.

**120Hz**
This is how many times per second the TV screen can refresh the image. A
higher number means smoother motion is possible, if the console is also
producing frames fast enough. A 120Hz TV is a "the screen can do it" number.
It does not mean every game, or GTA 6 specifically, will run at that speed.

**VRR (Variable Refresh Rate)**
Normally a TV refreshes at a fixed rate. VRR lets the TV adjust its refresh
rate on the fly to match whatever frame rate the console is actually putting
out at that moment. This reduces screen tearing, where the image looks
split or glitchy during fast motion, and stuttering. It is one of the more
genuinely useful features on this list for console gaming.

**Input lag**
This is the delay between you pressing a button on the controller and
seeing that action happen on screen. Lower is better. It matters most in
fast reactions, like braking a car or aiming quickly. Most TVs list an input
lag number in milliseconds for their game mode; lower than 15ms is
considered very good.

**Game mode**
Most TVs apply picture processing, like smoothing motion or enhancing
colour, that looks nice for films but adds delay. Game mode turns most of
that processing off, which lowers input lag. Almost every modern TV has a
game mode setting, usually found in the picture settings menu or triggered
automatically when a console is detected.

## OLED vs LED, honestly

This is the biggest decision and both options are genuinely good, so it
comes down to trade-offs rather than one being simply better.

| | OLED | LED / Mini-LED |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contrast and black levels | Excellent, each pixel lights itself | Good, but backlight can leak light around bright objects |
| Motion clarity | Very good, pixels respond almost instantly | Good, varies by model |
| Peak brightness | Lower than the best LED TVs | Can get brighter, better in a sunny room |
| Burn-in risk | Small but real risk with static images left on screen a long time | No burn-in risk |
| Typical price | Higher for the same screen size | Usually cheaper at the same size |

**On burn-in specifically:** OLED panels can, in rare cases, show a faint
ghost of an image that was left static on screen for very long, uninterrupted
periods, such as a news channel logo or a game HUD left up for many hours a
day, every day. Modern OLED TVs have features that reduce this risk, like
pixel shifting and screen savers, and normal gaming with varied content is
low risk. It becomes more of a concern only with extreme, repetitive use,
such as leaving a paused game with a bright static menu on screen for hours
at a time, day after day. If that sounds like your usage pattern, a good
LED or mini-LED TV avoids the question entirely.

For most people who play a mix of games and switch what is on screen
regularly, either type is a safe choice.

## A simple way to decide

1. **Set a screen size and room brightness first.** A bright room favours a
   brighter LED or mini-LED TV. A darker room lets OLED's contrast shine.
2. **Confirm HDMI 2.1 ports**, ideally at least one, so the console can use
   its full feature set now and in future games.
3. **Check for VRR support**, which is now common on mid-range and higher
   TVs from most major brands.
4. **Check the input lag number** for game mode in reviews, not marketing
   copy, since manufacturers do not always list it clearly on the box.
5. **Do not buy based on a GTA 6 specific claim.** No resolution, frame rate,
   or HDR format has been confirmed for the game, from any source.

## Screen size and viewing distance

TV size gets less attention than HDMI ports and refresh rates, but it changes
how good the picture actually looks in your room more than most other specs.
A general rule for console gaming is to sit at a distance of roughly one and
a half times the screen's diagonal size for a 4K TV, though personal
preference varies a lot.

- **A small room or a desk setup** usually suits a 42 to 48 inch TV or a
  large gaming monitor, sat closer, so detail stays sharp without needing to
  turn your head.
- **A living room with normal sofa distance** usually suits 55 to 65 inches,
  which is the most common size range for console gaming TVs at the moment.
- **Buying too large for the room** can make a lower resolution source, or
  compressed video like some streaming or online content, look softer than
  expected, since the same number of pixels is stretched over more screen.

None of this is specific to GTA 6. It is the same general advice that
applies to picking a good TV for any modern console game.

## Sound, and why a TV alone is often not enough

Most TVs, including expensive ones, have relatively weak built in speakers,
because the space for a large speaker is limited inside a thin panel. This
is worth thinking about alongside the picture, since a big open world game
like GTA 6 will likely have a lot going on in its sound design once
released, even though the specifics are not known yet.

- **A soundbar** is a simple, affordable upgrade over built in TV speakers
  and works for both games and films.
- **A gaming headset**, covered in more detail on this site's headset guide,
  is a good option for late night sessions where you do not want to disturb
  others in the house.
- **HDMI eARC**, a feature on most HDMI 2.1 TVs, allows higher quality audio
  to pass from the console through the TV to a soundbar or receiver with a
  single cable, which is simpler than older audio return setups.

## A note on smart TV extras

Many TVs marketed for gaming also include extra dashboard features, like a
game bar overlay showing frame rate and input lag in real time, or automatic
low latency mode switching, which turns on game mode automatically when it
detects a console is active rather than requiring a manual menu change every
time. These are genuinely convenient quality of life features for console
gaming in general. They do not change how GTA 6 itself performs, since that
is decided by the game and the console, not the TV's smart features.

## Second-hand and older TVs

Not everyone needs to buy a brand new TV to play GTA 6 well. A TV from the
last few years that already supports HDMI 2.1, even a mid-range model, will
likely handle a big 2026 console release just fine, since PS5 and Xbox
Series X|S themselves set the practical ceiling for what any game on those
consoles can output. An older TV without HDMI 2.1 will still run GTA 6,
just without access to features like VRR or higher frame rate modes, if the
game happens to offer them, which has not been confirmed either way.

## What is not known about GTA 6's own display support

Rockstar has not announced GTA 6's resolution, frame rate targets, HDR
format, or any performance versus quality mode split. Nothing about this has
been confirmed, tested, or shown officially as of this page's last update.
Anything you read stating exact numbers for GTA 6's graphics performance is
not an official Rockstar source and should be treated as unverified.
