## The short answer

**GTA 6 has been delayed twice.** It started with an autumn 2025 target, moved
to 26 May 2026, and then moved again to 19 November 2026, in an announcement
made around 6 November 2025.

That second date, 19 November 2026, is the one standing today. Nothing has
moved since that announcement.

## The full timeline

| Date target | Status | Announced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autumn 2025 | Original window given for the game | This was the first public target |
| 26 May 2026 | First replacement date | Announced after the autumn 2025 window was dropped |
| 19 November 2026 | Current, standing date | Announced around 6 November 2025 |

Read the table left to right as a story. Rockstar first pointed at autumn
2025. That window came and went without a date ever being locked in, and
Rockstar then named 26 May 2026 as the new target. That date also did not
hold. Around 6 November 2025, Rockstar and Take-Two announced the move to
19 November 2026, which is the date the game is still tracking toward as of
this page's last update.

Both moves were announced directly by Rockstar or its parent company,
Take-Two. Neither was a leak or a rumour picked up from an unnamed source.
That matters, because a delay announced by the publisher itself is far more
reliable than a date guessed at by outsiders.

## Why the second delay happened

Bloomberg reported the second delay, the one that pushed the game from
26 May 2026 to 19 November 2026. Rockstar's stated reason both times was that
it needed more time to finish the game to the standard it wanted. Rockstar
did not give a more detailed breakdown of what specifically needed more time,
such as a particular system, mission, or region of the map. No leak or report
has confirmed a more specific cause, so this page will not guess at one.

## Has Rockstar said it won't happen again

Take-Two chief executive Strauss Zelnick addressed the date directly and said
the company does not expect the game to be delayed again. That statement
came from Take-Two itself, on the record, which is about as strong a
reassurance as a company can give before a game actually ships. It is still
not a guarantee. Executives can only speak to their expectations at the time
they say something, not to events that have not happened yet. As of this
page's last update, no further delay has been announced since the move to
19 November 2026.

## Why big games slip, in plain words

Delays like this are common for large, open-world games, and the reasons are
usually simple, even when studios do not spell them out in detail.

A game this size is built by thousands of people across years, and every
system in it, driving, shooting, the map, the missions, the online mode,
has to work together at once. If one part is not ready, shipping on the
original date means shipping something broken, and studios with the money
and time to wait usually choose not to do that.

Testing a game of this scale also takes longer than most players expect.
Bugs that only show up after dozens of hours of play, or only on certain
hardware, or only when two systems interact in an unusual way, take real time
to find and fix. Rushing that process is how games ship broken, and a broken
launch costs a publisher far more in refunds, bad reviews, and lost trust
than a delay costs in patience.

There is also a business side to picking a date. Publishers look at what else
is releasing around the same time, when the biggest audience is likely to be
spending money, and when their own financial year lines up best with a big
release. A date can move for reasons that have nothing to do with whether the
game itself is finished.

None of this means every delay is justified, and players are right to be
frustrated by them. It does mean that a delay on a project this size is
ordinary, not a sign that something has gone unusually wrong. Most big
open-world games from most studios have slipped at least once before launch.

## What this means for you right now

As of this page's last update, 19 November 2026 is the date to plan around.
Pre-orders have been open since 25 June 2026, and preloading for digital
copies starts on 12 November 2026, one week before launch. If the date
changes again, this page will be updated the same day it happens, with a
source linked at the bottom, the same as every other page on this site.

## How to tell a real delay from a rumour

Both of GTA 6's delays were confirmed by Rockstar or Take-Two directly, not
by an unnamed insider or a leaked internal memo. That distinction matters a
lot in games coverage, where rumoured delays circulate constantly and rarely
turn out to be accurate. A reliable rule for any future GTA 6 date change is
simple. If the news does not trace back to a Rockstar Newswire post or a
direct Take-Two statement, treat it as unconfirmed, no matter how confident
the headline sounds.

## What a delay does and does not tell you

A delay on its own does not tell you whether a game will be good, bad,
buggy, or polished. It tells you that the studio decided the game was not
ready to ship on the date it had previously named. Some heavily delayed games
turn out excellent. Some games that shipped on schedule turn out broken.
There is no reliable link between how many times a game slips and how it
plays at launch, so it is worth resisting the urge to treat delay count as a
quality score in either direction.

## The bottom line

Two delays, both announced directly by Rockstar or Take-Two, moved the game
from an autumn 2025 target to 26 May 2026 to the current date of
19 November 2026. Take-Two's chief executive has said he does not expect
another delay. That is the honest state of things today, no more and no
less.
