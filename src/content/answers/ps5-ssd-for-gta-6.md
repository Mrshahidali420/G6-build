## The short answer

**GTA 6's install size has not been published anywhere official, so nobody
can tell you exactly how much storage you need.** What this page can do is
explain how PS5 and Xbox storage expansion actually works, so you are ready
whatever the real number turns out to be.

## Say it plainly: the install size is unknown

Rockstar has not released a file size, download size, or install size for
GTA 6, on PS5, Xbox, or anywhere else. There is no PC version announced
either, so there is no PC requirement to compare against. If you see a
specific gigabyte number attached to GTA 6 right now, in September 2026, it
did not come from Rockstar and should not be trusted as fact.

This matters because storage guides for unreleased games often invent a
number based on "similar" past titles. A previous Rockstar game's size tells
you nothing certain about this one. Treat every number you see before an
official announcement as a guess.

## How PS5 storage actually works

The PS5 ships with a fixed amount of internal SSD storage, part of which is
already used by the system software. Sony allows you to expand that storage
through an internal M.2 slot, using a compatible drive, without needing an
external box or cable.

The key requirements, in general terms, are:

- **Speed.** Sony requires a minimum read speed for the expansion drive to
  work properly. Slower consumer drives are not supported for PS5 game
  installs.
- **Size and form factor.** PS5 supports specific M.2 drive lengths. Not
  every physical M.2 drive on the market fits or is supported.
- **Cooling.** PS5 expansion drives generally need a heatsink, either
  built into the drive or fitted separately, because the internal bay runs
  hot.

Always check Sony's current official compatibility list before buying a
drive, rather than assuming any M.2 SSD will work. Buying an unsupported
drive is a common and avoidable mistake.

## How Xbox storage expansion differs

Xbox Series X and Series S do not use a standard M.2 slot for expansion.
Instead, Microsoft sells an official proprietary expansion card that plugs
into a dedicated port on the back of the console. It is simple to install,
but it only works with cards built for this specific format. A generic M.2
drive that works in a PS5 will not work in an Xbox Series console, and vice
versa. The two ecosystems are not interchangeable, so buy the storage
product made for your specific console.

## External storage: useful, but limited

Both PS5 and Xbox let you connect external USB storage to hold games you are
not actively playing. This is a genuinely useful way to manage a large
library without buying expensive internal expansion.

The limitation to know about: on PS5, most external USB drives can store PS5
games but cannot run them directly. You need to move a game back to internal
storage or a compatible M.2 expansion drive before you can actually play it.
Xbox has similar rules for some titles. Treat external storage as an
archive, not as a place to install and play GTA 6 from directly, unless your
specific console and drive combination is confirmed to support that.

## Why "just buy the biggest drive" isn't automatically the right answer

It is tempting to solve an unknown by simply buying the largest storage
option available. That is not wrong exactly, but it is worth being honest
about the trade-off. Larger M.2 drives and larger expansion cards cost
noticeably more per gigabyte at the high end. If GTA 6 turns out to need a
moderate amount of space, which is entirely possible even though it is not
confirmed, you may have paid a premium for headroom you did not need. The
more measured approach is to buy enough to comfortably cover your current
shortfall and a reasonable buffer, then reassess once the real number is
public, rather than buying the maximum size out of uncertainty alone.

## Managing your existing library in the meantime

Whatever you decide about buying new storage, freeing up space you already
have costs nothing. A few general, low-effort options:

- **Uninstall games you have finished or are not currently playing.** Most
  are quick to reinstall later if you change your mind, especially on a fast
  SSD.
- **Move rarely played titles to external USB storage**, keeping in mind the
  PS5 limitation that most externally stored PS5 games need to move back to
  internal or expansion storage before you can play them again.
- **Check for large media files or captures** taking up space, such as saved
  screenshots and video clips, which can add up over time without you
  noticing.

None of this requires spending money, and it is worth doing before launch
week regardless of whether you also buy expansion storage.

## A practical plan, without guessing at a number

Since nobody can tell you the exact GTA 6 install size yet, here is what is
actually useful to do:

1. **Check how much free space you have right now.** If your console is
   already nearly full, you have a storage decision to make regardless of
   what GTA 6 needs.
2. **Decide if you want to buy expansion storage now or wait.** Buying now
   avoids a last-minute scramble. Waiting until closer to launch means you
   can buy based on the real number instead of a guess.
3. **Watch for preload.** Preloading for digital pre-orders starts 12
   November 2026, a week before the 19 November 2026 release. Exact download
   sizes are typically listed on the PlayStation Store and Xbox Store once
   preload opens, and that is the moment to check the actual figure rather
   than trust a rumour.
4. **Use the official store listing, not a forum post, to check the size
   once it appears.** Storefront numbers come directly from the publisher.
